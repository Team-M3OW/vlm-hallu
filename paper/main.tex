\begin{abstract}
Vision--language models decide \emph{what to look at} --- which visual tokens to keep, where to crop
--- by reading their own attention. We show the field reads it at the wrong depth.
Question-conditioned localisation does not exist in the vision encoder (at chance on four
architectures), is too weak at early language-model layers to be usable, and becomes reliable only in
the upper half of the stack. The practical cost is large and, to our knowledge, unreported: ranking
visual tokens for pruning by layer-2 attention --- the default in a widely used method --- is
\bad{worse than ranking them at random} on both models we test, losing $22.0\pp$ and $13.1\pp$
against no pruning at all. Reading late layers instead discards \gd{90\% of visual tokens at no
measurable cost} ($-0.5\pp$ and $-1.6\pp$), a margin of $+24.1\pp$ and $+11.5\pp$ over the
early-layer default, with no training. The same defect appears in crop placement: replacing the
standard block-mean read-out with a learned one improves evidence localisation on three of four
architectures spanning two model families, and question answering by $+12.0\pp$ and $+10.5\pp$ over
an unmodified model. A logit-lens analysis shows why cropping helps at all: it does not improve
reasoning but restores an answer-formation step that appears abruptly at a single layer, and only
when the crop actually delivers the evidence. We report what does not work with equal weight,
including seven internal interventions that all fail and five of our own claims rejected by
cross-architecture replication.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A vision--language model (VLM) turns an image into a few hundred visual tokens. When the question
concerns something small, the evidence occupies a fraction of one token and the model cannot answer:
below roughly $0.15$ merged tokens of target extent we measure $19.0\%$ accuracy --- \emph{below the
$25\%$ chance level of a four-way question} --- while the identical question asked of the resolved
region is answered correctly $96$--$100\%$ of the time.

Two large families of methods respond to this, and both make a decision from the model's attention.
Token pruning discards visual tokens judged unimportant. Crop-and-re-encode methods select a region
and look again. \textbf{Both read attention, and the depth at which they read it has been treated as
an implementation detail.} It is not.

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pruning at the wrong depth is worse than not ranking at all.} Ranking visual tokens by
layer-2 attention scores \emph{below random selection} on two architectures. A late read-out at the
same budget retains the accuracy of using every token while discarding $90\%$ of them
(Section~\ref{sec:prune}).
\item \textbf{The standard read-out averages away its own signal.} Collapsing a block of layers into
one map and taking the maximum is worse than a learned combination on three of four architectures,
two model families (Section~\ref{sec:readout}).
\item \textbf{A mechanism, with a causal control.} Cropping does not improve reasoning: cropped and
uncropped trajectories are indistinguishable for twenty layers and then separate abruptly --- and the
separation appears \emph{only} on items where the crop actually contains the evidence
(Section~\ref{sec:mech}).
\item \textbf{An audited account of failure.} Seven internal interventions that would have been free
if they worked, all null; and five claims of our own rejected when tested on a second architecture
(Section~\ref{sec:neg}, Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup}

All experiments use V*Bench ($n{=}191$ four-way questions with ground-truth boxes) and, for transfer,
HR-Bench 4k ($n{=}800$, CircularEval, $4032^2$ images). Models: Qwen3-VL-2B, Qwen2-VL-7B,
LLaVA-OneVision-7B, LLaVA-NeXT-7B. \textbf{Token counts are always measured} from the processor's
realised grid, never computed; any arm drifting more than $10\%$ from its target voids its contrast.
Every learned component is fitted \textbf{out-of-fold with folds grouped by item}.
Appendix~\ref{app:proto} gives the full protocol.

\section{The read-out defect}\label{sec:readout}

The standard way to locate evidence in a VLM is to average attention over a block of layers and take
the argmax. Figure~\ref{fig:defect} shows why this is lossy: layers disagree about where the target
is, and a mean cannot represent disagreement.

\section{Pruning at the wrong depth}\label{sec:prune}

Token pruning ranks visual tokens by attention and drops the low-ranked ones. The widely used default
reads that attention at layer 2. Figure~\ref{fig:prune} shows the consequence. Every arm prunes the
same number of tokens, so cost is matched by construction.

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig3_pruning.pdf}\end{center}
\caption{\textbf{Ranking at layer 2 is worse than ranking at random.} Dashed line: keeping every
token. A late read-out discards $90\%$ of visual tokens at no measurable cost, on both models.}
\label{fig:prune}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_prune_masks.pdf}\end{center}
\caption{\textbf{What each ranking actually throws away.} Unshaded regions are the $10\%$ of visual
tokens kept; everything darkened is discarded before the answer is computed. Yellow box: the queried
object. Beneath each panel is the model's \emph{real} prediction on that pruned input. Items are the
first three in our pool on which the two rankings give different answers --- selected on
\emph{disagreement}, never on which ranking turns out to be right, and on the first item both are
wrong. Across the full pool of 14, late-layer pruning preserves the full-image answer on
\gd{13/14} items and layer-2 pruning on \bad{7/14}.}
\label{fig:masks}
\end{figure}

At $10\%$ keep, the late read-out is within noise of using every token ($-0.5\pp\ci{-5.8}{+4.7}$ and
$-1.6\pp\ci{-6.3}{+3.1}$), while layer-2 ranking loses $22.0\pp\ci{-29.8}{-14.7}$ and $13.1\pp$
respectively and falls \emph{below random selection} on both models ($-3.7\pp$, $-4.7\pp$). The
margin is $+24.1\pp\ci{+16.2}{+31.9}$ and $+11.5\pp\ci{+4.2}{+18.8}$.

\textbf{No learning is required.} A plain average of late layers matches a trained signed combination
at every operating point ($+2.6$, $-1.0$, $+1.0\pp$, all null). The recommendation is a one-line
change of which layer index is read.

\paragraph{A prediction that failed.} We pre-registered that the penalty would be \emph{larger} on
Qwen2-VL, whose early layers rank the target worse. It is \emph{smaller} ($-13.1$ vs $-21.9\pp$).
Layer ranking quality therefore does not predict pruning damage: the claim replicates, its causal
story does not, and we state them separately rather than let one carry the other.

\section{Mechanism: what cropping actually does}\label{sec:mech}

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig4_l21.pdf}\end{center}
\caption{\textbf{Cropping restores an answer-formation step; it does not improve reasoning.} Left:
the answer read out at every layer. Right: the same gain split by whether the crop contained the
evidence.}\label{fig:l21}
\end{figure}

Reading the model's answer at every layer (Figure~\ref{fig:l21}) separates two explanations. A method
that improved reasoning would pull ahead early and widen. Instead the cropped and uncropped arms are
\emph{indistinguishable through L20}, then separate by $+15.3\pp$ at L21 and stay separated. The
failing arms jump at L2 --- the answer prior forming --- and never improve: the uncropped arm's best
score across all 28 layers \emph{equals} its final answer, so there is no depth at which the answer
was available and lost.

The right panel is the causal control. Splitting the \emph{same} arm by whether its window contained
the evidence: $+29.7\pp\ci{+19.8}{+39.6}$ where it covers, $-7.9\pp\ci{-18.0}{+2.2}$ where it misses
--- a $37.6\pp$ swing. The step appears only when the intervention delivers.

\paragraph{Replication.} Both the shape and the control hold on Qwen2-VL-7B. Separation is
${\approx}0$ through L21 and then steps ($+8, +16, +13, +12, +12, +10\pp$ at L22--L27); the index
moves by one layer, which we did not require to transfer. The control replicates at
$+23.8\pp\ci{+13.1}{+34.5}$ where the window covers against $+0.0\pp\ci{-11.2}{+11.2}$ where it
misses, a $23.8\pp$ swing. One detail does \emph{not} generalise: on Qwen3-VL a miss is mildly
harmful ($-7.9\pp$) whereas on Qwen2-VL it is exactly neutral, so the cross-model claim is that the
gain appears \emph{only} where the crop delivers, not that a miss actively hurts. The sharpest
sub-claim holds identically on both: the uncropped arm's maximum over all 28 layers \emph{equals}
its final answer ($56.3\%$ and $50.8\%$), so on neither model is there a depth at which the answer
was available and lost.

\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_crops.pdf}\end{center}
\caption{\textbf{The crops the model is actually fed, and what it actually answers.} Top: the source
image with the queried object (yellow) and the window each read-out selects. Below: the real cropped
input handed to the model, refit to the same $300$ tokens, with its prediction and confidence.

\section{What does not work}\label{sec:neg}

Seven internal interventions, each free if it worked, are all null: suppressing the serialisation
sink, amplifying attention on the target, residual-stream steering, layer read-out selection,
DoLa/DeCo, component ablation, and contrastive decoding \emph{steered to the ground-truth region}
(ceiling $+2.6\pp\ci{-0.5}{+5.8}$). The last is decisive because it aimed perfectly and verified the
target is causally live --- masking the evidence region shifts logits $3.32\times$ more than masking
a random region and costs $10.5\pp$ --- and still could not convert it.

\begin{quote}
The model looks in the right place; the right place is load-bearing; it still cannot answer, because
no operation on the residual stream can supply information the encoder never wrote.
\end{quote}

Four attempts to \emph{regress a continuous geometric quantity} from internals also failed (budget
from a perfect size oracle; budget from any free signal; window size from target size;
attention-space reallocation). Ranking positions is learnable from internals; regressing scale is not.

\section{Limitations}

Our crop-placement method improves over an unmodified model on two architectures
($+12.0\pp\ci{+4.2}{+19.9}$, $+10.5\pp\ci{+2.6}{+18.3}$) but \textbf{we have not shown it beats
simply spending the same compute on a larger uniform image} ($+4.7\pp\ci{-3.7}{+13.1}$ and
$+3.1\pp\ci{-5.2}{+11.0}$). We report this rather than restricting to the stratum where it clears.
Pruning baselines are reimplemented on a common backbone, not run from released checkpoints.
Coverage labels require ground-truth boxes and so exist only on V*Bench. Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}
tags every claim by how many models support it.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{app:proto}

\subsection{Budgets are measured, never computed}
A visual-token budget cannot be set analytically: processors merge patches, pad, and round. Every arm
is therefore calibrated by iterating on the processor's \emph{own realised grid} until the token count
is within tolerance, and the realised count is logged with every record. Any contrast whose arms
differ by more than $10\%$ in median realised tokens is \textbf{voided} rather than reported. In the
runs reported here all arms land within $0$--$6.7\%$ of target.

\subsection{Numerical precision}
Models load in \texttt{bfloat16}. Loading a bf16 checkpoint in fp16 produces all-NaN logits; because
\texttt{argmax} over NaN returns index $0$ and the first option is over-represented in the label
distribution, this reads as a clean, plausible, uniformly negative result. It cost us a spurious
architecture-specificity finding before it was caught. All scripts now assert \texttt{torch.isfinite}
on logits and attention before writing any record.

\subsection{Cross-validation}
Every learned component uses grouped $k$-fold ($k{=}5$) with folds grouped \emph{by item}, so an
item's ${\sim}300$ cells never straddle train and test. Layer-selection results are reported
out-of-fold: in-sample selection of a ``best layer'' is the best of 28 candidates on 191 items and is
badly optimistic (Appendix~\ref{app:corr}).

\subsection{The logit lens}\label{app:lens}
Intermediate layers are read as $\texttt{lm\_head}(\texttt{norm}(h_i))$; the \textbf{final} layer is
taken from \texttt{model.logits} and \emph{never} from $\texttt{norm}(h_{-1})$. HuggingFace has
already applied the final norm to the last hidden state, so re-applying it gives reconstruction error
$23.47$ against $0.06$ --- corrupting only that layer, which makes the error invisible in layer-wise
plots. Both paths are computed and their disagreement is asserted, so a zero gap now signals that the
\emph{correct} path is not in use.

\subsection{Statistics}
All intervals are paired bootstrap over items, $10{,}000$ resamples, percentile method. Where a
prediction is stated before a run it is written into the script's docstring with its rejection rule,
so a result cannot be reinterpreted after the fact. We report intervals rather than $p$-values
throughout, and we do not correct for multiplicity: the claims that survive are those that replicate
on a second architecture, which is a stronger filter than any correction.

\section{The allocation ledger: what we could not make work}\label{app:alloc}

Four attempts to \emph{regress a continuous geometric quantity} from model internals, all failed:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Token budget from target size.} With a \emph{perfect} ground-truth area oracle, the rule
$b \geq t^*/\textrm{area}$ loses to flat uniform in 11 of 12 cells, scored against the uniform ladder
at each controller's own mean spend.
\item \textbf{Token budget from any free pass-1 signal.} 17 features (attention concentration, entropy,
spread; logit-lens confidence, margin, cross-layer agreement; image geometry). Out-of-fold
$\rho = 0.254$, but exact-rung accuracy is $9.4\%$ against a $35.6\%$ majority baseline --- worse than
a constant. The required-budget distribution is bimodal ($68/191$ items need the smallest budget,
$37$ need more than the ladder offers) and no free signal makes that call.
\item \textbf{Window size from target size.} Across four quartiles spanning a $100\times$ range of
target area, the best window is $0.15$ in \emph{every} quartile. A per-item oracle window is worth
$+11.0\pp$ after a stability check, so the headroom is real --- but no predictor we tried reaches it.
\item \textbf{Attention-space reallocation.} Amplifying attention on the \emph{ground-truth} cells
recovers $16\%$ of what cropping the same region buys. The target is sub-token: there is nothing to
amplify.
\end{itemize}

\begin{quote}
Ranking positions is learnable from internals. Regressing scale is not. Every method in this paper
that works is a ranking method, and every one that failed asked the model how \emph{much} rather than
\emph{where}.
\end{quote}

\section{Rank-only multi-crop: a falsified prediction}\label{app:multicrop}

The ledger above suggests a method that only ranks: take the top-$k$ separated cells, split the budget
evenly, show all $k$ in one pass --- no size or budget decision anywhere. We priced it before running:
union coverage of the top-4 \emph{and} above the encoding cliff at $75$ tokens each is $63.4\%$
against single-crop's $52.9\%$, i.e.\ $+10.5\pp$ of answerable items at identical total budget,
turning over at $k{=}5$. At the measured coverage exchange rate this predicts $+5.5\pp$.

Measured: $\mathbf{-3.1\pp\ci{-9.4}{+3.1}}$. The prediction had the wrong \emph{sign}, so the model
behind it is wrong, not merely imprecise. The exchange rate was estimated on \emph{single} crops,
where coverage decides whether the evidence is visible at all; with four crops, three are distractors
and the account has no term for distractor cost. Ranking still works inside the format --- our top-4
beats four random crops by $+27.7\pp$ --- but the format costs more than the extra coverage buys.

This contradicted an earlier in-project result of $+7.9\pp$ for multi-crop, so before recording the
negative we tested the clearest difference between the two setups: the connector text between images.
Re-running with the descriptive connector gave \emph{identical} accuracy on all three arms, while
$0/25$ probability vectors matched byte-for-byte --- the prompt changes the output without flipping
decisions. The deficit is real, not a prompting artifact.

\section{Seven internal interventions}\label{app:seven}

Each would have been free if it worked; each is null.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Serialisation-sink suppression.} Biasing attention away from the trailing row boundary
is indistinguishable from biasing away random or interior image tokens ($-0.5\pp$), while the same
operation on \emph{prompt} tokens costs $-17.3\pp$ --- so the machinery bites, and image-token
attention is simply not scarce in the direction of removal.
\item \textbf{Attention amplification on the target.} Ground-truth-targeted amplification gains
$+5.8\pp$, $16\%$ of what cropping the same region buys. Every practical arm is indistinguishable
from a random control.
\item \textbf{Residual-stream steering.} A shared direction exists (cosine $0.53$--$0.75$) and an
item's own displacement is $47\%$ reachable at L20, but the transferable vector gives $+0.0\pp$.
\item \textbf{Layer read-out selection for the answer.} No layer under uniform encoding exceeds
$52.9\%$ against the oracle crop's $92.6\%$.
\item \textbf{DoLa / DeCo.} Null against a correctly-computed baseline. (Against the
double-normalised baseline of Appendix~\ref{app:corr} they appeared favourable, which is how the bug
was found.)
\item \textbf{Component ablation.} Null.
\item \textbf{Contrastive decoding steered to the ground-truth region.} The decisive one, because it
aims perfectly. Masking the evidence region shifts logits $3.32\times$ more than masking a random
region and costs $10.5\pp$ of accuracy --- the region is unambiguously load-bearing --- and yet
contrast on it reaches a \emph{ceiling} of $+2.6\pp\ci{-0.5}{+5.8}$, and $-5.5\pp$ against its own
compute-matched bar.
\end{enumerate}

The seventh retires the family. The previous six could each be dismissed as steering to the wrong
place; this one steered to the ground truth, verified the place was causally live, and still could
not convert it.

\section{Corrections made during this work}\label{app:corr}

We list these because several changed conclusions, and because the mechanism that caught each one is
the part worth reusing.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A double-normalised logit lens} (Appendix~\ref{app:lens}) manufactured a ``$+5.1\pp$
free read-out gain'', an evidence-dependent late-layer bias, and a favourable comparison against
DoLa. \emph{Caught} because a second code path computed the same quantity and disagreed --- $100\%$
agreement at L24, $82.2\%$ at the final layer.
\item \textbf{A transfer result called negative from a prefix.} HR-Bench is ordered by category; the
first $244$ rows were $65\%$ relational, the stratum where our method does nothing. The balanced
$n{=}800$ result reversed the sign ($+0.0 \rightarrow +4.9\pp$, CI clear of zero).
\item \textbf{Two mechanisms proposed for a failure that did not exist.} Both were refuted by our own
data --- ``the map is flat at 4K'' (peak concentration $0.0228$ vs $0.0269$, only $1.18\times$ apart)
and ``below the cliff there is nothing to re-rank'' (the gain is \emph{largest} below the cliff).
\item \textbf{A capture fraction against a restricted ceiling.} Quoted as $65\%$ of an
oracle-among-top-5 gap; against the true ceiling (does \emph{any} cell cover: $88.5\%$) it is
$27.6\%$. The loose version also implied the method was near its limit when $35.6\pp$ of headroom
remained.
\item \textbf{An AUROC computed on an unmasked map} ($0.576$); ring-masked as everywhere else in the
paper it is $0.745$. This briefly made a second signal look competitive when it is substantially
worse.
\item \textbf{In-sample layer selection} read $41.9\%$ and collapsed to $36.1\%$ out-of-fold ---
\emph{below} the block mean it appeared to beat, inverting the conclusion for that model.
\item \textbf{A pre-registered mechanism prediction that failed} (Section~\ref{sec:prune}): the
layer-2 pruning penalty should have been larger on the model whose early layers rank the target
worse. It is smaller. The claim replicates; the causal story does not, and we report them separately.
\end{itemize}

An executable suite of \textbf{86 invariants} encodes every item above, so a claim the data stopped
supporting fails a test rather than surviving in prose.
