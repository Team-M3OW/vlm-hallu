\title{Re-spend the Tokens Where the Model Already Looks:\\Depth-Profile Re-ranking for Small-Object VQA}

\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{abstract}
Vision--language models (VLMs) fail on small objects because the evidence is never encoded, not
because attention is misdirected. On \vstar, $87\%$ and $84\%$ of two models' errors are fixed by
spending the \emph{same} visual-token budget on the evidence region instead of the whole image; an
oracle crop is flat at $96$--$100\%$ across a threshold of ${\approx}0.25$ merged tokens of target
extent below which uniform encoding falls under the $25\%$ chance level. Seven internal interventions
cannot cross that threshold: amplifying attention on exactly the right cells recovers $16\%$ of what
re-encoding those cells recovers, because the oracle set is one cell of $294$. We give a method that
does cross it. \emph{Depth-Profile Re-ranking} reads the language model's attention over image cells
at all $28$ layers, ranks cells with a small learned head, and crops once. Against the equal-compute
baseline of encoding a larger image, it is the only arm in our study that wins, and it wins on two
architectures and two benchmarks on single-object questions ($+15.7$, $+11.3$, $+7.5$, $+8.5\pp$,
all CIs clear); the standard argmax read-out clears in no cell. Along the way we show the deployed
read-out is wrong on four separable axes --- it averages layers where a max is worth $+7$\pp, reads
the attention weight rather than $\alpha\|v\|$, averages all heads, and fails entirely unless the
prompt ends at the answer-emission point --- and that ranking visual tokens by layer-2 attention is
worse than ranking them at random on two models and three benchmarks. We report the method's three
measured limits (relational questions, existence questions, one architecture whose layers agree),
a ${\approx}91$-parameter readable head at parity with the learned one, a label-free rule within
$3$\pp\ of it, and every negative --- including four of our own claims rejected by replication and a
six-week-old negative overturned by one line of a prompt.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A VLM that is asked ``what colour is the glove?'' and answers wrongly can fail in two ways: it can
attend to the wrong place, or it can attend to the right place and find nothing there. A growing
family of methods assumes the first --- highlighting evidence pixels~\citep{liu2026vea}, rescaling
attention heads~\citep{lu2026reallocating}, supervising attention during
training~\citep{esmaeilkhani2026klal} --- and all report gains. None measures how large the queried
object is. We do, and on the benchmark built for small objects the second failure dominates: the
model localises, the region is causally load-bearing, and the tokens covering it carry nothing the
answer needs.

The practical consequence is that the only fix is to \emph{re-spend the token budget}: encode the
region the model already points at, at the resolution the budget affords. That is not a new idea ---
attention-guided cropping is a family of its own~\citep{zhang2025vicrop,shen2024zoomeye,attwarp2026}
--- but as far as we can find, no member of it has been scored against the one baseline that
decides whether it earns its second forward pass: \emph{spend the same tokens on a larger image}.
When we run that comparison, every published policy we test breaks even or loses
(\S\ref{sec:priorart}). Ours is the only arm that wins, and the reason is a read-out, not a policy.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{A method that beats equal compute (\S\ref{sec:method}).} Depth-Profile Re-ranking
(DPR) ranks image cells by their full $28$-layer attention profile and crops once. On single-object
questions it beats a uniform image at the same token count on \vstar\ and \hrb, on Qwen3-VL-2B and
Qwen2-VL-7B: $+15.7$, $+11.3$, $+7.5$, $+8.5\pp$, all confidence intervals clear of zero. The
deployed argmax clears in no cell. Zero-shot to \hrb\ with nothing refitted.
\item \textbf{The strong baseline it is measured against (\S\ref{sec:readout}).} The standard
read-out --- mean over a late block of layers, mean over heads, argmax --- is wrong on four axes,
each fixable in one line. We port the fixes from LLM work (max over layers~\citep{mcdanel2026claa},
$\alpha\|v\|$~\citep{kobayashi2020norm}, head selection~\citep{lu2026reallocating}) and find they do
not stack; the layer \emph{set} does. A label-free rule --- max over the layers where attention is
question-conditioned --- is within $3.7/2.7\pp$ of the learned head. Ranking tokens by layer-2
attention is \bad{below random} on two models and three benchmarks.
\item \textbf{Why it works (\S\ref{sec:why}).} Below ${\approx}0.25$ merged tokens the evidence is
not encoded: $87\%/84\%$ of errors are oracle-fixable, the oracle is flat across the threshold, and
seven internal interventions fail. Cropping restores an answer-formation step at layer $21$, only
where the crop delivers.
\item \textbf{Where it stops (\S\ref{sec:limits}).} Three boundaries, each with a mechanism and each
found by our own controls: relational questions (the evidence set is a union $7.9\times$ larger),
existence questions (a crop is evidence of absence; POPE's oracle is circular), and one architecture
whose layers agree with each other. The learned read-out is task-specific: $3{,}000$ public boxed
items from a text-reading task train a head \emph{worse than no head}.
\end{itemize}

Everything is pre-registered where a selection was possible, replicated on a second model before it
is claimed, and reported with the negatives at equal weight (\S\ref{sec:neg}, App.~\ref{app:corr}).

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} \vstar~\citep{wu2024vstar}: $191$ four-way questions on ${\sim}1500$--$2000$px
images whose targets are small by design; ground-truth boxes; $115$ single-object
(\texttt{direct\_attributes}) and $76$ relational (\texttt{relative\_position}). \hrb~\citep{wang2024hrbench}:
$800$ rows / $200$ instances at $4032$px, no boxes, evaluated per row and under CircularEval (all
four option cycles correct). POPE~\citep{li2023pope} with RePOPE labels~\citep{neuhaus2025repope}
and COCO boxes ($482$ relabelled, $812$ dropped as ambiguous) for the scope test of
\S\ref{sec:limits}; MMBench~\citep{liu2024mmbench} for pruning only; $3{,}000$ TextVQA
items~\citep{singh2019textvqa} with evidence boxes from Visual-CoT~\citep{shao2024viscot} for
\S\ref{sec:limits}. Public benchmarks only; nothing constructed.

\paragraph{Models.} Qwen3-VL-2B and Qwen2-VL-7B (all end-task claims); LLaVA-NeXT-7B and
LLaVA-OneVision-7B for read-out and mechanism claims (their \texttt{image\_newline} separators are
stripped and the grid rebuilt, App.~\ref{app:proto}). Frozen weights throughout; bf16.

\paragraph{Budget accounting.} Every arm's visual-token count is \emph{measured} from the
processor's \texttt{image\_grid\_thw}, never computed; a contrast whose arms drift by more than
$10\%$ is void. The method costs two $300$-token passes; its baseline is one $600$-token pass
(``uniform@600'', the bar). Reported drifts are $0.1$--$1.3\%$.

\paragraph{Read-out and statistics.} Answers are read from the option logits, no generation.
Confidence intervals are $95\%$ item-level bootstraps ($\geq 4000$ resamples); a claim is made only
if its CI clears zero on both Qwen models. Every learned quantity is out-of-fold, \texttt{GroupKFold(5)}
grouped by item so an item's ${\sim}300$ cells never straddle folds; selections are made on training
folds only. The run-to-run floor on top-1 coverage is $1$--$2.5\pp$ (App.~\ref{app:proto}).

\section{The method: Depth-Profile Re-ranking}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Localise.} Encode the image at $B_0{=}300$ merged tokens with the full question. From that
pass, take the final token's attention over the $n$ image cells at every one of the $28$ language-model
layers, head-averaged and sum-normalised per layer: a $28\times n$ profile. \emph{The prompt must end
at the answer-emission point} (\S\ref{sec:readout}); this is the whole of what makes the map usable.

\paragraph{Rank.} Score each cell with $65$ features from that pass alone: its $28$ depth values,
its $28$ within-layer ranks, a $3\times3$ neighbourhood mean, the deployed block-mean, and seven
geometry terms including explicit last-column / last-row / first-column indicators so the head
\emph{can} learn the serialisation sink (App.~\ref{app:sink}) if that is what matters. The scorer is a
gradient-boosted regressor (depth $4$, $150$ trees) trained to predict \emph{coverage} --- the fraction
of the evidence box a window centred on the cell would contain --- out-of-fold, grouped by item.
Border cells are ring-masked, as in the deployed proposer.

\paragraph{Spend.} Crop a window of side $W$ of the image around the top cell, re-encode at $300$
tokens, answer. $W{=}0.25$ on \vstar\ (held-out validated: folds pick it $99/100$, in-sample
$71.7\%\to$ held-out $71.6\%$); $W{=}0.15$ unswept on \hrb.

\paragraph{The gap it exploits.} On \vstar\ the deployed argmax covers the evidence $40.5\%$ of the
time; oracle selection among the \emph{same map's} top-5 covers $61.5\%$; the ground-truth cell already
sits in the top $3.4\%$ of cells. The ranking has the answer and the max throws it away, usually to a
sink or a distractor (Fig.~\ref{fig:crops}).

\subsection{Internal controls}
On the rows where the head and the argmax select the same cell (84 of 800 on \hrb), the two arms are
the same computation and their contrast is exactly $+0.0\pp$; a non-zero value there would void the
headline. Re-running the head arm in a separate script reproduces the stored value on $100\%$ of items
on both models. The learned head's gains are not geometry: a head given only position scores $3.7\%$
coverage and a head trained on shuffled labels $2.6\%$, both at the rate of a random cell.

\section{What the method is measured against}\label{sec:readout}

The deployed read-out --- mean over layers $16$--$26$, mean over heads, argmax --- is the baseline
this literature uses, and it is weak for four separable, nameable reasons. Reporting DPR only against
it would overstate the head's contribution by a factor of three (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig1_defect.pdf}\end{center}
\caption{\textbf{The same forward pass, read at two depths.} A real \vstar\ item: attention over
image cells at layer $2$ (left) and in the late band (right), with the ground-truth box. The early
map is not noisy --- it is a confident map of something that is not the question. Reading it is
worse than not reading anything (Table~\ref{tab:prune}).}
\label{fig:defect}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Axis 1: rule.} CLAA~\citep{mcdanel2026claa} diagnoses layer-wise ranking instability for
LLM prefill with an answer-informed oracle and aggregates by \emph{max} across a window of layers.
Ported here: max over the deployed block is $+7.3\ci{+2.6}{+12.6}$ / $+6.8\ci{+3.1}{+11.0}\pp$ of
coverage on the two Qwen models; their $4$-layer window-max, window chosen out-of-fold, $+8.4/+12.0$.
Mean over all layers and median are both \emph{worse} than the block mean.

\paragraph{Axis 2: quantity.} \citet{kobayashi2020norm} showed in BERT that attention to special
tokens is high-$\alpha$, low-$\|v\|$. Our serialisation sink (App.~\ref{app:sink}) is the same
object: weighting each cell by $\alpha\|v\|$ is $+4.7\ci{+2.1}{+7.9}\pp$, and composes with the max
to $+10.5\ci{+5.8}{+15.7}$ with no training and no selection.

\paragraph{Axis 3: heads.} Keeping the top $10\%$ of heads by their visual-attention
ratio~\citep{lu2026reallocating} and taking the max is $+11.5\ci{+5.8}{+17.8}\pp$, label-free.
CoRe's~\citep{tran2026core} contrastive criterion, selected out-of-fold, is $+3.1\ci{-1.0}{+7.3}$
over it --- not significant, and it needs boxes; the absolute criterion that fails in text works here.

\paragraph{Axis 4: prompt.} The read-out is the final token's attention. If the prompt does not end
at the answer-emission point the map is unusable: with the answer options present but the
instruction line removed, the argmax collapses to $3.7\%/0.5\%$ coverage --- below the ${\sim}2\%$
chance of hitting the target cell --- while removing the options and keeping the instruction changes
nothing ($47.6/41.4$ vs $46.1/39.8$). This one line is why every \hrb\ negative in an earlier
version of this work was wrong (App.~\ref{app:corr}), and it plausibly explains published
``random beats attention'' grounding nulls~\citep{wen2025rightproblem}.

\paragraph{The fixes do not stack; the layer set does.} Norm weighting, head selection and max each
beat the deployed rule, but composed they land on the same ${\sim}56/{\sim}49\%$: they remove one
sink by three routes. The component that adds on top is \emph{which layers}: replacing the hand-set
block with the layers where attention is question-conditioned --- measured label-free by how much
the map changes under four unrelated questions (\S\ref{sec:mech}) --- gives $59.7/51.8\%$, within
$3.7/2.7\pp$ of the learned head (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). The gate is threshold-robust ($0.3$--$0.7$
select the same layers, L17--20 on Qwen3-VL and L19--22 on Qwen2-VL) and independently re-finds
Qwen2-VL's answer-formation layer.

\paragraph{What does not transfer, and why that is a result.} Attention rollout and
gradient$\times$attention --- the standard faithfulness fixes in text --- collapse to $12.6\%$ and
$15.7\%$, below the deployed rule. ReAttn's entropy rescaling is catastrophic in both directions.
\emph{Fixes that target the sink transfer; fixes that model information mixing destroy the signal.}
Rollout multiplies $28$ attention matrices with residual identities and the query-conditioned spatial
signal, which exists only in the late half, is washed out by the early layers it must pass through.

\paragraph{No training-free rule clears the bar on both models.} End-task, the best label-free rule
is $+10.4\ci{+0.9}{+20.9}\pp$ over equal compute on Qwen3-VL single-object questions but
$+7.0\ci{-2.6}{+16.5}$ on Qwen2-VL; the CLAA window-max is $+9.6/+6.1$. One of two, so under our rule
it is not a bar-clearing method; it is the honest no-training counterpart, and the learned head
leads it by $4$--$6\pp$ with lower bounds at or just under zero.

\subsection{Ranking visual tokens by early attention is below random}\label{sec:prune}

The same read-out is the ranking signal in attention-based token pruning, where the widely used
default reads layer $2$~\citep{chen2024fastv}. We pin the prune point at layer $2$ for every arm ---
identical budget, identical savings --- and move only the layer the ranking is read from. This
decomposition is, to our knowledge, unrun: every surveyed method moves read depth and prune depth
together (\S\ref{sec:related}).

Table~\ref{tab:prune} is the paper's most widely replicated result: two models, three benchmarks,
and the effect is \emph{larger} on general VQA than on the small-object benchmark that might flatter

read beats random by $17.8\pp$ when pruned early. Both hold, because read depth and prune depth are
two knobs. A pre-registered mechanism prediction failed here and is reported as failed: Qwen2-VL's
early layers rank the target \emph{worse} (gt-pct $0.620$ vs $0.456$) yet its layer-2 penalty is
\emph{smaller} ($-13.1$ vs $-21.9\pp$); ranking quality does not predict pruning damage.

\subsection{A readable head at parity}\label{sec:readable}

The gradient-boosted tree is accurate but opaque. Four rounds of replacement --- an MLP, cross-attention
over the layer axis, a spatial CNN over the $28$-channel map with MSE and pairwise losses, additive
GAMs, rank fusion, ensembles --- fail to beat it on both Qwen models (App.~\ref{app:head}); the
constraint is $191$ boxed items, not model class. What does reach parity is a Lasso-fit log-linear
model with ${\sim}91$ parameters: $\sum_l w_l \log A_l + \sum_l v_l\,\mathrm{rank}_l + \sum_l u_l
\log \overline{A}^{3\times3}_l + $ geometry. At the deployed $W{=}0.15$ it is $+2.6/+1.6\pp$ over
the tree on Qwen3/Qwen2 (CIs span zero), $+4.7$ on LLaVA-NeXT and $+6.3\ci{+1.6}{+11.5}$ on
LLaVA-OneVision --- the one model the tree fails on --- and it clears the deployed argmax on four of
four architectures where the tree manages three. Its weights say what the tree learned: L19 strongest
on both models, L21 on Qwen2-VL (its answer-formation layer, \S\ref{sec:why}), the last layers
\emph{subtracted} on Qwen3-VL, neighbourhood terms carrying $1.5$--$2.3\times$ the mass of point
terms, sink indicators ${\approx}0$. Rank fusion alone loses $25\pp$: the magnitudes carry the
signal, which is also why norm weighting helps and rollout fails. We use the tree for the numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:method} and the readable head to say what they mean.

\section{Why it works: the evidence is not encoded}\label{sec:why}

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_coverage_cliff.pdf}\end{center}
\caption{\textbf{Left:} coverage of the evidence set by the crop window decides the \emph{sign} of the
allocation effect, crossing zero near $25\%$; half of all windows miss. \textbf{Right:} the encoding
cliff. Uniform encoding falls below the $25\%$ chance line under ${\approx}0.25$ merged tokens of
target extent while the oracle crop is flat at $96$--$100\%$ across it, on both models.}
\label{fig:cliff}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The cliff.} Define tokens-on-target as the target's area fraction times the visual budget.
Below $0.25$, uniform encoding scores $32.4\%\ci{21}{44}$ and $36.6\%\ci{25}{48}$ on the two models
--- on the cleanest single-region subset, $19.0\%$, under the $25\%$ chance level --- while the oracle
crop at the same $300$ tokens scores $95.8\%$ on both. Above it, $77.3/63.6\%$ against an oracle at
$100\%$. The oracle arm is the control: it is flat across the split, so the items below the threshold
are not harder, they are invisible. Stated as \citet{li2026lastpixel}'s RT$_{50}$ in our units, the
threshold is ${\approx}0.15$--$0.25$ merged tokens on both checkpoints; their synthetic-canvas
threshold for Qwen3-VL-2B converts to ${\approx}0.14$--$0.39$.

\paragraph{Nine in ten errors are encoding failures.} Of every \vstar\ error, the oracle crop fixes
$86.7\%$ (Qwen3-VL) and $84.0\%$ (Qwen2-VL) at $W{=}0.25$, and $94.0/89.4\%$ at $W{=}0.15$. Same
model, same question, same budget; only the placement differs.

\paragraph{Seven interventions fail, two of them decisively.} Activation steering, sink suppression,
residual-stream injection, DoLa/DeCo~\citep{chuang2024dola}, component ablation, attention
amplification and contrastive decoding are all null (App.~\ref{app:seven}). The two that close the
argument: amplifying attention on \emph{exactly} the right cells recovers $+5.8\pp$ against the crop's
$+36.2\pp$ at the same budget --- $16\%$ --- because the oracle amplification set is one cell of $294$;
there is nothing to amplify. And the region is causally live: masking it moves the output $3.32\times$
more than a random region and costs $10.5\pp$, yet contrastive decoding steered to it has a ceiling of
$+2.6\ci{-0.5}{+5.8}$. The model looks in the right place; the right place is load-bearing; it still
cannot answer, because no operation on the residual stream supplies information the encoder never
wrote.

\paragraph{What the crop restores.} Read the answer at every layer (Fig.~\ref{fig:l21}). The cropped
and uncropped arms are indistinguishable through layer $20$, then separate abruptly. Split the same
method by whether its window actually contained the evidence: $+29.7\ci{+19.8}{+39.6}$ where it
covered and $-7.9\ci{-18.0}{+2.2}$ where it missed on Qwen3-VL; $+23.8\ci{+13.1}{+34.5}$ and
$+0.0\ci{-11.2}{+11.2}$ on Qwen2-VL. Same computation, same budget, same question; only coverage
differs. Coverage is the mediator and it decides the sign (Fig.~\ref{fig:cliff}): a missed window costs
$-15.6\pp$, a fully covering one gains $+37.1\pp$, and within a single difficulty stratum the gap is
$70.8\pp$ with missed windows at $13.0\%$, below chance. The method pays exactly where the cliff says it
should: $+13.8\pp$ below $0.15$ tokens, $+16.0$ in the cliff zone, $+3.7$ (n.s.) above.

\paragraph{Against the predecessor's reading.} \citet{liu2026vea} conclude from the same kind of
attention maps that VLMs \emph{see but do not believe}: they attend to evidence they fail to use.
That holds above the threshold. Below it, the correct option is decodable at \emph{no} layer on
items the model localises but answers wrongly ($72.2\%$ ever-argmax vs $68.1\%$ for items whose window
missed entirely --- no difference) and the oracle crop fixes $94.4\%$ of them. The model points but
never saw. The two accounts divide at the cliff, and neither VEA, \citet{lu2026reallocating} nor
\citet{esmaeilkhani2026klal} measures target size or runs the oracle control that locates it; the
last is the honest exception, and it works by changing the weights --- its own ablation shows
target-token norms rising $6$--$19\%$.

\section{Where it stops}\label{sec:limits}

Every limit below was found by a control we ran, and each has a mechanism.

\paragraph{Relational questions.} DPR loses on them in every cell (Table~\ref{tab:method}). The
evidence set of a relational question is the \emph{union} of the objects involved, $7.9\times$ larger
in area than a single-object target; one window cannot cover it, and a perfect crop of one region
\emph{wins} ($+19.7\pp$), so it is coverage and not question difficulty. Predicted from the coverage
account before any run. This is the first claim in the project to replicate across two models and two
benchmarks, and it is a limit.

\paragraph{Existence questions.} On POPE the crop arm is $-56.7$, $-77.3$ and $-63.0\pp$ against the
bar on the three positive strata, because it answers ``no'' to almost everything: for ``is there an $X$
in the image?'' a window showing $6\%$ of the area is genuine evidence of \emph{absence}. The
attention localiser is at chance there ($9.2\%$ centre-in-box vs $10.7\%$ expected) --- existence
questions do not demand localisation --- and the oracle arm is \emph{circular}: knowing the box is
knowing the object is present. So POPE cannot supply a ceiling, and crop-based allocation is invalid,
not merely unhelpful, for questions that do not presuppose their target. The pre-registered prediction
that cropping would \emph{raise} false positives was wrong in direction (they fell $4.0\pp$), for the
same reason.

\paragraph{One architecture whose layers agree.} On LLaVA-OneVision the learned head is
$-1.0\ci{-6.3}{+3.7}$ and max-over-layers is $-3.7$: it is the one model where the best single layer
\emph{equals} the block mean, so there is no disagreement across depth for any re-ranker to exploit.
The head is $3$ of $4$ architectures on proposals ($+14.1$, $+8.9$, $+7.3\ci{+2.1}{+12.6}$); the
readable head of \S\ref{sec:readable} is $4$ of $4$.

\paragraph{The read-out is task-specific.} The obvious lever against a $191$-item ceiling is more
boxed data. We trained the head on $3{,}000$ TextVQA items with public evidence boxes and applied it
zero-shot to \vstar: $38.2\%$ coverage at $W{=}0.15$ and $37.2\%$ at $W{=}0.25$, \bad{below the
deployed argmax} ($39.3/46.6$), and mixing them into the \vstar\ folds degrades the head
($-5.8\ci{-11.0}{-1.0}$). The same head beats the argmax on TextVQA itself ($46.5$ vs $38.3$), so it
learns; what it learns does not carry. The reason is visible in the layer profiles: question-conditioned
localisation switches on at L11--13 for text reading and at L16 for small-object attributes, so a head
trained on the former trusts layers that are still question-blind on the latter. Transfer works
within a question type (\vstar\ $\to$ \hrb) and fails across --- the VLM counterpart of
\citet{orgad2025llmsknow}'s ``skill-specific'' probes, and of \citet{lu2026reallocating}'s
task-dependent boundary bands.

\section{Mechanism}\label{sec:mech}

\paragraph{Attention is question-blind until half depth, on four architectures.} Run the same image
under four unrelated questions and measure per-layer divergence of the attention map --- no labels.
It is $0.001$--$0.005$ through L12 on both Qwen models, then jumps $13\times$ by L16 ($0.137/0.178$);
the switch-on is at L14/32 ($44\%$ depth) on LLaVA-NeXT and L16/28 ($57\%$) on LLaVA-OneVision. This
is the band CLAA, \citet{lu2026reallocating} (their transition zone, $37$--$63\%$ depth) and VEA
(L16--26) independently identify --- four measurements, two modalities. The locator finds the
\emph{region}, not the best layer: on Qwen2-VL divergence rises at L13--14 but pruning damage recovers
at L14--16, and reading at L14 still loses $6.3\pp$ to L16 (App.~\ref{app:layers}).

\paragraph{Localisation is built by the language model.} Every read of the vision tower's $24$ layers
is at or below the $2.3\%$ chance rate (best layer $1.6\%$, learned combination $1.0\%$, mean $0.5\%$)
while the language model reaches $39.3\%$ on the same items. The encoder never sees the question.
Attention from the \emph{noun naming the target} is near chance (gt-pct $0.367$ vs $0.500$):
localisation is not lexical lookup and exists only after the whole query is integrated, at the answer
position (\S\ref{sec:readout}).

\paragraph{Attention quality and answer formation are dissociated.} Attention localises best at
L17--19; the answer forms at L21; rank correlation across layers $\rho{=}0.297$. Attention top-1 is
\emph{higher} in the six layers before the answer forms ($30.1\%$) than after ($24.8\%$). Raised by an
external reviewer, tested, confirmed: these are two quantities, not one.

\paragraph{The serialisation sink.} The image is flattened row by row; the sink sits at the row
boundaries. Last column $5.1\times$ its share of attention, first column $2.9\times$, rows $0.9/0.8\times$
--- columnar, not cornered. A transpose test ($14\times21$ vs $21\times14$) keeps the sink at the same
(row, col) positions with only $4/8$ shared absolute indices, so these are not register tokens; replaying
sink indices into the interior lands \emph{below} chance ($1.1\%$ vs $3.3\%$). The prediction only this
account makes: models that splice a learned \texttt{image\_newline} at each row end put the sink
\emph{on the separator} ($2.0$--$2.3\times$), which is not a corner, not a pixel and not image content.
Four architectures, two families (App.~\ref{app:sink}). Suppressing it buys nothing; biasing \emph{any}
$4.8\%$ of image tokens moves accuracy by $\leq 2\pp$ while the same number of text tokens costs $17\pp$.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Attention reweighting} --- VEA~\citep{liu2026vea}, head rescaling~\citep{lu2026reallocating},
KLAL~\citep{esmaeilkhani2026klal} --- reports gains, measures no target size and runs no oracle control;
\S\ref{sec:why} locates the family's boundary. VEA finds L16--26 by AUROC on document VQA; we find the
same band label-free on small-object search, a convergence we cite rather than claim.
\textbf{Cropping and allocation} --- ViCrop~\citep{zhang2025vicrop}, ZoomEye~\citep{shen2024zoomeye},
AttWarp~\citep{attwarp2026}, Visual-CoT~\citep{shao2024viscot}, CropVLM~\citep{carvalho2026cropvlm} ---
is scored at matched realised budget in App.~\ref{app:priorart}: every policy breaks even or loses
(ZoomEye to a single uniform image of its own budget; grounding $-6.2\pp$ at median IoU $0.029$; AttWarp
$+0.6$; tree search at 4K $-15.3$ at $2.2\times$ the tokens, $n{=}800$), and so does our own fixed
always-crop policy; only the learned read-out passes.
\textbf{Token pruning} --- FastV~\citep{chen2024fastv}, FEATHER~\citep{endo2025feather},
TwigVLM~\citep{shao2025twigvlm}, LearnPruner~\citep{learnpruner2026}, \citet{wen2025rightproblem},
\citet{wang2026worsethanrandom} --- establishes that early-layer attention is unreliable; none runs the
read-depth/prune-depth decomposition of \S\ref{sec:prune} or reports the four axes of \S\ref{sec:readout}.
\textbf{Interpretability transfer} --- rollout~\citep{abnar2020rollout}, norms~\citep{kobayashi2020norm},
LRP~\citep{chefer2021beyond}, CLAA~\citep{mcdanel2026claa}, CoRe~\citep{tran2026core},
ReAttn~\citep{tian2026reattn}, \citet{orgad2025llmsknow} --- is ported and scored in \S\ref{sec:readout}
and App.~\ref{app:probes}. \textbf{Fine-scale perception}: \citet{li2026lastpixel} find perception
saturating near $12$px on synthetic canvases; the cliff is its natural-image, token-unit,
oracle-controlled counterpart.

\section{What does not work}\label{sec:neg}

Reported at equal weight because it is most of what we learned. Each entry has a pre-registered rule
applied as written; details in App.~\ref{app:negatives}.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Per-item window size.} Pre-registered, dead on two models and two grids: $-3.1\pp$
against the fixed constant it was meant to improve.
\item \textbf{Gates.} The two-pass confidence rule that rescued the old proposer is null for the head
($-0.5$); a free routing gate clears on one model only and was barred by pre-registration from
substituting for the sizer.
\item \textbf{Multi-crop.} Four ranked windows in one pass: predicted $+5.5$, measured $-3.1$; the
coverage model has no term for distractor cost.
\item \textbf{More capacity.} MLP, cross-attention over layers, spatial CNN, GAM, ensembles: nothing
beats the tree on both models; a $91$-parameter model matches it.
\item \textbf{More features.} Giving the head the max-aggregate and $\alpha\|v\|$ signals that rescue
the simple read-out adds nothing: it already extracts them.
\item \textbf{More data.} $3{,}000$ cross-task boxed items train a head worse than no head.
\item \textbf{Pre-generation detection.} The attention depth profile does not detect certified encoding
failures ($1$ of $2$, ordering reversed); plain predictive entropy is the best detector on both models.
\item \textbf{A token-level cliff.} Evidence tokens were at chance below the cliff on one model
($0.513$ vs $0.656$ above) and not on the other ($0.623$ vs $0.633$); withdrawn.
\item \textbf{Two of our own diagnostics}, refuted by the measurements that were meant to confirm them.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}

The end-task win is single-object questions on two Qwen checkpoints and two benchmarks; pooled it does
not clear anywhere. LLaVA is tested on proposals only. The cliff, the interventions and the mechanism
are \vstar-only because it is the only venue with boxes where the oracle control is valid ---
POPE's oracle is circular, \hrb\ has no boxes --- and five of the seven intervention nulls are
single-model. The head is task-specific and needs boxes on its training benchmark. The equal-compute
bar counts tokens, not latency; two $300$-token passes and one $600$-token pass are not identical in
wall-clock. Every number in this paper was obtained with $n{=}191$ or $n{=}800$ items and a
$1$--$2.5\pp$ run-to-run floor on coverage; lower bounds of $+1.7$ and $+2.2$ are real but thin.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{app:proto}
\paragraph{Budgets are measured, never computed.} Visual-token counts are read from
\texttt{image\_grid\_thw} after processing; images are iteratively resized to hit a target (tolerance
$6\%$). A contrast whose arms drift by $>10\%$ is void. Qwen3-VL's processor floors every image at
$64$ merged tokens, so tiling arms below $5\times64$ are infeasible rather than mismatched.
\paragraph{Localisation pass.} The full prompt --- question, options where they exist, and the answer
instruction --- with the image at $300$ tokens; last-token attention over image cells, head-averaged,
each layer sum-normalised. Border cells ring-masked before any argmax. LLaVA models: \texttt{image\_newline}
separators are detected by matching the model's own embedding against the merged sequence, stripped,
and the grid rebuilt from the modal row length; a naive split once swallowed the separator-free base
image as ``row 0'' and put $59.8\%$ of positions in a bucket that can hold ${\sim}5\%$.
\paragraph{Answer read-out.} Softmax over the option letters' logits at the final position; yes/no over
the \texttt{Yes}/\texttt{No} token families. No generation, so results are deterministic.
\paragraph{The logit lens.} Intermediate layers: \texttt{lm\_head(final\_norm(h\_i))}. The final layer
must be read from \texttt{model.logits}: HuggingFace returns \texttt{hidden\_states[-1]} already
normalised, and normalising it again corrupted only the last layer, manufacturing a $+5.1\pp$ ``free
read-out gain'' and three sections we retracted (App.~\ref{app:corr}); the two implementations agreed
on L24 for $100\%$ of items and on the final layer for $82.2\%$, which is how it was found.
\paragraph{Cross-validation.} \texttt{GroupKFold(5)} grouped by item, three fold-permutation seeds,
training rows negative-subsampled to every covering cell plus $30$ random non-covering cells per item;
evaluation on all cells. In-sample layer selection reads $41.9\%$ on Qwen3-VL and $36.1\%$ out-of-fold
--- below the block mean it appeared to beat --- which is why nothing here is selected in-sample.
\paragraph{Statistics and the noise floor.} Item-level bootstrap, $4000$--$8000$ resamples. The same
head on the same items under two attention extractions differs by ${\sim}1\pp$; under two fold
seedings by up to $2.5\pp$. No coverage difference inside that band is reported as signal.
\paragraph{Numerical precision.} Qwen2-VL loaded in fp16 produces all-NaN logits; \texttt{argmax}
falls through to index $0$, and because $71/191$ \vstar\ labels are ``A'' every arm scores an identical
$37.2\%$ with CI $[+0.0,+0.0]$. Two false negatives that looked exactly like findings; bf16 throughout,
and an arm contrast of exactly $+0.0\,[+0.0,+0.0]$ is treated as a pipeline fault, never a null.

this is reported as a measurement of the blind-cut tolerance, not as a method.

\section{The training-free ladder}\label{app:trainfree}
All fixed constants unless marked. Block L16--26 (Q3) / L15--26 (Q2); $q{=}0.10$; divergence gate
$\geq 0.5\cdot\max$, robust across $0.3$--$0.7$. Components tested and rejected: ReAttn entropy
rescaling ($-19$ to $-27\pp$ on norm-weighted maps, $-2$ to $-8$ on raw --- early layers are diffuse
and any entropy reweighting promotes them); VEA's $3{\times}3$ outlier denoising at $\lambda{=}10$ ($-1$
to $-3\pp$ on Qwen; isolated high-value cells on \vstar\ are usually the target, the opposite of document
VQA); leave-one-out background normalisation (null-to-negative once max/heads are present).
End-task at $W{=}0.25$, single-object vs uniform@600, head arm re-run as control ($100\%$ agreement):
learned head $+15.7/+11.3$; label-free gated max $+10.4\ci{+0.9}{+20.9}$ / $+7.0\ci{-2.6}{+16.5}$;
composite $+9.6/+7.8$; CLAA window-max $+9.6/+6.1$. Norm-weighted max end-task on Qwen3-VL (ph.\,113):
$68.6\%$ pooled, not clearing. Cross-family: no training-free rule helps LLaVA; max hurts OneVision
($-4.2$); the divergence \emph{mechanism} replicates there but gating by it does not rescue localisation.

\section{Prior art at matched budget}\label{app:priorart}\label{sec:priorart}
\vstar, Qwen3-VL-2B, every arm scored against the uniform sweep interpolated at its own realised
token count. ZoomEye~\citep{shen2024zoomeye}: highest raw accuracy in the table, loses to one uniform
image of its own budget. Grounding (``locate the $X$ and output its box''): $-6.2\pp$, boxes parse on
$98\%$ of items with median IoU $0.029$, because it must ground on the same downscaled view that cannot
resolve the target. AttWarp~\citep{attwarp2026}: same attention source, same inverse-CDF warp, scored
against the bar its paper never runs: $+0.6\pp$, break-even, and it costs two passes. Our fixed
always-crop policy: $60.2\%$ against a $66.0\%$ bar, captures $10\%$ of the oracle gain; a conditional
allocator answering from the more confident pass clears it ($68.6\%$), and its learned gates lost to a
rule. At 4K ($n{=}800$): tree search $-15.3\pp$ against its own bar and $-10.4$ against uniform@1200 at
$2.2\times$ the tokens, worst arm per token; grounding flips sign across scales ($-6.2\to+1.3$) exactly
as the coverage account predicts. Gating recovers $+5.7\pp$ for ZoomEye while cutting its tokens $53\%$:
tree search wastes most of its nine passes on items no crop helps. The exchange rate: no uniform rung
reaches the crop arm on \vstar\ up to the model's ceiling, a lower bound of $\geq 26\times$ set by the
architecture; on LLaVA-NeXT there is no budget axis to move along at all.

\section{Seven interventions}\label{app:seven}
All on Qwen3-VL unless stated; one pass at identical tokens, so no bar to clear. (1) Activation steering
at L16, direction fit on disjoint items: null; a \texttt{rand\_last} control raised $P(\text{yes})$ by
shifting bias, after which every arm reports a false-positive rate. (2) Sink suppression: indistinguishable
from suppressing random or interior tokens; suppressing the same number of prompt tokens costs $17.3\pp$.
(3) Attention amplification on the oracle cells: $+5.8$ vs the crop's $+36.2$ at the same budget; every
practical arm (top-1/5/15, deployed window) indistinguishable from random. (4) Residual-stream injection:
a shared ``allocate to evidence'' direction exists (cosine $0.53$--$0.75$) and is answer-irrelevant
($+0.0\pp$ at every layer for the mean direction; an item's own displacement at L20 recovers $47\%$;
L26 recovers everything and is near-tautological). (5) DoLa dynamic $+0.0$, DeCo best-of-8 $-0.7$,
read-at-L24 $+0.0$, with their hyper-parameters swept in their favour. (6) Zeroing attention or MLP in
L25--27: $\leq 2.4\pp$, never significant. (7) Contrastive decoding steered to the predicted region
$+2.1$, to the ground-truth region $+2.6\ci{-0.5}{+5.8}$, after verifying the region is causally live
($3.32\times$, $-10.5\pp$ masked). Vision-tower localisation: best single layer $1.6\%$, learned
combination over $24$ layers $1.0\%$, mean $0.5\%$, chance $2.3\%$.

\section{Probes and the Orgad port}\label{app:probes}
Hidden states at five positions $\times$ $28$ layers, OOF logistic probes, size-matched random-region
control mandatory (without it a probe separates ``annotated object'' from ``random rectangle'' and once
read AUROC $1.000$; retracted). Target \texttt{err}: final position $0.714/0.657$, evidence tokens
$0.587/0.626$, random region $0.503/0.516$. \citet{orgad2025llmsknow}'s exact-answer-token claim does
not port: evidence tokens are $0.127$ and $0.031$ \emph{worse} than the final position. The token-level
cliff ($0.513$ below vs $0.656$ above on Qwen3-VL) did not replicate ($0.623$ vs $0.633$ on Qwen2-VL)
and is withdrawn. Evidence-token information peaks at L1 and decays to chance by L27 on Qwen3-VL. The
pre-generation detector from the attention depth profile: AUROC $0.630$ vs baselines $0.588/0.625$ on
Qwen3-VL, $0.579$ vs $0.651/0.679$ on Qwen2-VL; predictive entropy wins on both.

\section{Negatives in full}\label{app:negatives}
\paragraph{Window sizer.} Pre-registered five-value grid, OOF per-item $W$ from six free pass-1
features. Qwen3-VL $+8.9\ci{+0.5}{+17.3}$ over the bar on the five-value grid, inverting to
$+6.8\ci{-1.6}{+14.7}$ and $-1.0$ against the fixed constant on the three-value grid; Qwen2-VL on the
pre-registered grid $+3.7\ci{-3.1}{+10.5}$ vs bar and $-3.1$ vs the constant, sending $43/191$ items to
the worst window. Dead. The \texttt{top1\_frac} gate ($+9.6\ci{+2.0}{+17.7}$ on Qwen3-VL) was barred by
pre-registration from substituting.
\paragraph{Multi-crop.} $k{=}4$ windows at $B_0/4$ each in one pass: $+7.9\pp$ over top-1 on \vstar\ with
the argmax proposer and $+9.5$ over the bar on single-region, transferring to 4K ($+8.0$) --- but
rank-only multi-crop with the head is $-3.1$ against a predicted $+5.5$ (prompting confound excluded),
and the 4K win was measured with the crippled localiser (App.~\ref{app:corr}).
\paragraph{Budget from a free signal.} No pass-1 signal predicts the required budget: $9.4\%$ exact-rung
against a $35.6\%$ majority baseline. \texttt{peak} predicts \emph{coverage} at AUROC $0.788$ but
correctness at $0.50$; random routing at the same cost already earns $+3.3\pp$ from the concavity of the
uniform curve, so \texttt{peak}'s own contribution is $+1.9$.
\paragraph{Top-$k$ verification.} The head's top-2 recovers $14$--$17\%$ of top-1 misses ($+4.8/+5.9$
coverage $\approx +3$--$4\pp$ accuracy) while a third pass moves the bar to uniform@900 ($\approx+4\pp$);
top-2 is adjacent to top-1 on $67\%$ of items. Predicted null; not run. Misses are distractors (median
$3$--$5$ cells away, $5$--$8\%$ near-misses), so spatial fixes cannot help.
\paragraph{Two refuted diagnostics.} Both were proposed to explain a 4K failure that turned out to be an artefact. ``Allocation wins where the budget axis has saturated'':
\vstar's axis is near-linear ($+8.0$, $+7.3$, $+8.7\pp$ per doubling). ``The crop must be re-encoded at
native resolution, so $W^\ast\approx 485/\text{side}$'': predicted $0.12$ at 4K, measured $0.25$, flat on
single-object.

\section{Corrections made during this work}\label{app:corr}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Every \hrb\ phase before the final one localised on the bare question.} Eight scripts,
cloned from the first, passed the question with the options and the answer instruction stripped. On
identical rows with both uniform arms agreeing on $100\%$ of predictions, restoring the full prompt moves
the crop arm by $+19.3\pp$ and changes the argmax cell on $55\%$ of items. Every 4K negative in an
earlier version --- the transfer negative, 4K gating, our arm in the 4K prior-art table, four ``attempts
to win at 4K'', the scale sweep, 4K multi-crop --- is withdrawn; the prior-art losses stand (they run their
own localisers). The scale boundary we spent five phases explaining was a prompt bug. Rule: never clone a
localiser; import one.
\item \textbf{The double-normalisation bug} in our logit lens (App.~\ref{app:proto}) invalidated three
sections, including ``DoLa/DeCo beaten by a free read-out''; corrected, no layer-decoding rule beats the
model's own logits.
\item \textbf{An ROI-selection artefact} produced a probe AUROC of $1.000$ on the cohort the model
answers wrong; the control that exposed it is now mandatory.
\item \textbf{A capture-fraction mispairing} would have inverted a conclusion ($83\%\to88\%$ matched;
the mismatched pairing reads $95\%$).
\item \textbf{``Signed contrast is the mechanism''} and \textbf{``the final layer is anti-correlated''}
were Qwen3-VL-specific and are not claimed.
\item \textbf{A void phase} was built on the belief that the head read only L16--26; it read all $28$.
\item \textbf{A void run} patched Qwen3-VL's attention module while running Qwen2-VL; all arms identical,
CI $[+0.0,+0.0]$.
\item \textbf{The token-level cliff} (App.~\ref{app:probes}) was called this paper's best result before
its second model; it failed there and is withdrawn. Four single-model results failed replication in one
session; none is characterised as strong before its second model.
\item \textbf{RePOPE:} confident model disagreement is a $7.2\times$ enriched label-error detector, and
every finding strengthens on the clean pool.
\end{enumerate}
