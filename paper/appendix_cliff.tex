\section{The size cliff in detail}\label{app:cliff}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Every measurement in this appendix uses \vstar\ with its ground-truth boxes, on
Qwen3-VL-2B and Qwen2-VL-7B unless stated. A \emph{merged visual token} is one post-merge patch
embedding; at a $300$-token pass it covers $1/300$ of the image area. A target's extent is $S\times
B$ merged tokens, where $S$ is the ground-truth box area as a fraction of the image and $B$ the
realised token count; its side is $\sqrt{S\times N}$ tokens. The \emph{oracle crop} replaces the
model's window with the ground-truth box at the same $300$-token budget and is a control, not a
method. An item is a \emph{certified encoding failure} when it is wrong under the uniform bar and
right under the oracle crop at the same budget. \emph{Coverage} is the fraction of the ground-truth
box inside the $W{=}0.25$ window at the ring-masked arg-max of a read-out. The \emph{exchange rate}
compares the oracle crop at the base budget with the best uniform rung each model can reach.
Thresholds come from a sliding sweep: for each $t$, accuracy below and at-or-above $t$ is compared on
single-region items, requiring at least ten items per side, and $t^*$ maximises the step. All
intervals are paired item bootstraps ($8000$ resamples).

\paragraph{The cliff.} Accuracy as a function of the target's extent, with the oracle crop as the
difficulty control, is the central measurement of the section. The uniform curve is flat and near
chance for the smallest targets, dips \emph{below} the $25\%$ chance level, and rises steeply
through the shaded $0.15$--$0.25$ merged-token band; the oracle curve is flat at
$95.8\%$/$100.0\%$ across the same range. The two models' curves differ in level but not in shape or
in where the step sits. The step is sharpest at $t=0.15$ on the cleanly-localised single-region
subset, where the intervals are disjoint, and it appears at the same $0.25$-token threshold on all
single-region items of both models.

\paragraph{It is not the budget.} The natural objection is that the cliff is simply a budget effect:
spend more tokens and the target stops being sub-token. It is not. Recomputing the sliding threshold
at $300$ and $588$ tokens shows that the threshold in merged tokens scales with the budget
($0.25\to0.55$ on Qwen3) while its image-side fraction is unchanged ($2.89\%\to3.06\%$); Qwen2 is
noisier but agrees in direction. In other words, the criterion is the target's \emph{relative size},
not the number of tokens that land on it: at a fixed tokens-on-target, accuracy differs between the
two budgets (bin $[0.15,0.25)$: $33.3\%$ vs $53.8\%$ on Qwen3; $41.7\%$ vs $61.5\%$ on Qwen2), so the
two budgets do not collapse onto one curve. Uniform budget cannot substitute for magnification
because it leaves the target's fraction of the image unchanged.

\paragraph{The information is never encoded.} If the answer were present but mis-routed, a layer-wise
read-out would find it somewhere and lose it later. We decode the answer option from every layer
with a logit lens: intermediate layers through $\mathrm{lm\_head}(\mathrm{final\_norm}(h_\ell))$,
the final layer from the model's own logits, because applying the final norm to the last hidden
state as well double-normalises that layer alone and corrupted an earlier result of ours
(\S\ref{app:mech}); we assert per run that the last-layer read-out matches the true logits. Under
the uniform encoding no layer's decoded answer exceeds $52.9\%$, and localisation quality does not
change that: on items where the window covers the target and the model still answers wrong
($n{=}36$), the correct option is the argmax at some layer on $72.2\%$ of items, against $68.1\%$
where the window missed entirely. There is no layer at which the answer is present and subsequently
lost: the apparent signal at L20 ($41.7\%$) collapses to $0\%$ from L22 and matches what near-chance
layers produce by chance. Under the oracle crop the lens instead reaches $92.6\%$, and the answer
appears abruptly at L22, taking $90\%$ of the gain in a single transition; the step replicates one
layer later on Qwen2-VL-7B and appears only where the crop covers the target ($+23.8$ points
covered, exactly $0.0$ where it misses). Cropping does not make the model reason better; it supplies
information that a fixed depth converts into an answer. Representation patching agrees from the
other side: replacing every image token's hidden state with a different image's, at a single layer,
changes the answer no more than replacing irrelevant early text once past the transport boundary (KL
$0.009$ vs $0.009$ on Qwen3 and $0.005$ vs $0.006$ on Qwen2 at L16). The oracle crop fixes $94.4\%$
of the localised-but-wrong items at the same budget, with a median of $0.14$ merged tokens on
target, and across the benchmark $37.7\%$/$41.4\%$ of all items are certified encoding failures:
$86.7\%$/$84.0\%$ of every error, rising to $94.0\%$/$89.4\%$ at $W{=}0.15$.

\paragraph{Allocation beats budget.} The exchange rate compares an oracle crop at the base budget
with the best uniform rung each model can reach; a random crop of the same size bounds the placement
effect from below. No uniform rung reaches the crop arm on any model, so every rate is a lower bound
set by the model's token ceiling; LLaVA-NeXT has no resolution axis and is reported as such. The
same statement holds at native resolution, where the uniform arm may spend $5.5\times$ our budget: a
perfectly placed $600$-token crop beats native dynamic resolution ($3{,}290$ tokens) by
$+11.6$/$+16.2$ pooled. The boundary is measured rather than assumed: allocation pays when the
evidence is smaller than the budget can resolve --- $71\%$ of \vstar\ targets are sub-token and
oracle allocation is worth $+37.2$ points --- and costs when it is not, as on HR-Bench 4K, where
allocation loses $8.8$ points because discarded context dominates. The localizer is good in both
regimes; what changes is whether acting on it helps.

\paragraph{Why the target is invisible.} The arithmetic behind the threshold is simple. At the
$300$-token pass the median single-region target's side is $0.210$ merged tokens; $100\%$ of targets
are smaller than one merged token, the median target occupies $0.176$ tokens of area, and $62\%$ sit
below the $0.25$-token cliff. The evidence is averaged into a single token's footprint together with
its background before the language model ever sees it --- which is why magnification, not budget, is
the operation that recovers it.

\paragraph{Controls.} Three controls bound the interpretation. \emph{Localisation degrades below the
cliff, far less than accuracy}: ground-truth coverage falls from $0.637$ to $0.457$ (argmax window)
and from $0.751$ to $0.580$ (head window) on Qwen3, and $0.594\to0.450$ / $0.620\to0.471$ on Qwen2,
while accuracy falls by $42.6$ points --- encoding dominates, placement contributes a minority
share. \emph{Magnification is not the bottleneck}: the tightest oracle window tested, $W{=}0.15$,
resolves $100\%$ of items on both models in every size quartile (median $W^*=0.15$; rank correlation
with $\sqrt{S}$ $\approx0$), so what limits the model is isolating the region and the target's
relative size, not the amount of magnification. \emph{The below-chance failure is not the language
prior}: a text-only control (question without the image) agrees with the image-conditioned answer on
$49.4\%$ of below-cliff items vs $40.7\%$ above on Qwen3, but $49.4\%$ vs $56.5\%$ on Qwen2 ---
opposite directions, neither interval clear. Below the cliff the model is systematically misled, but
not by the text-only prior.
